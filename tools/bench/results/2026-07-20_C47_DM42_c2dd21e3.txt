Observations from this run:
- Against the DM42n at matched USB power the DM42 is a near uniform 2.5x
  slower: every benchmark lands between 2.35x and 2.79x, a spread of only
  1.19x. A single scalar therefore relates the two devices on USB, which
  is what makes the host simulator usable as a stand-in for either.
- That scalar does NOT survive the move to battery. On battery the same
  device ratio spreads from 2.26x to 4.61x. Cross-device predictions
  should be made at matched USB power only.
- The DM42's own battery penalty is markedly non-uniform, 2.08x to 4.23x,
  and wider than the DM42n's 1.86x to 3.22x. BMVAR is the outlier at
  4.23x: the linear user-variable scan is the most memory-bound work in
  the suite and it suffers most when the clock drops.
- The inflation is not an artefact of the cell sagging over a 24 minute
  run. Correlation between a benchmark's battery/USB ratio and its
  position in the suite is +0.05, i.e. none; BMDISP runs last and has one
  of the lowest ratios.
- The lookup diagnostic (BMVAR - BMREG) / (BMRSV - BMREG), per iteration,
  is 4.48x on USB and 3.95x on battery here, against 4.71x and 4.19x on
  the DM42n and 4.87x on the host. The linear scan costs about five times
  the hashed path in every environment measured so far (upstream #555).
- The battery clock is 24 MHz against 80 MHz on USB, a ratio of 3.33x, but
  the suite only slows by about 2.1x on most benchmarks. Per clock the CPU
  does roughly 1.55x more work at 24 MHz (median over the suite). The DM42n
  shows no such effect going 160 MHz to 80 MHz, where the median is 1.02,
  i.e. it scales almost exactly with the clock. Consistent with flash wait
  states: the L476 needs 4 of them at 80 MHz and 1 at 24 MHz, and the ART
  accelerator only partly hides that at full speed. Stated as inference
  about the mechanism; the effect itself is measured.
- BMVAR is the exception on both devices, slowing 4.23x on the DM42 and
  2.56x on the DM42n against clock ratios of 3.33x and 2.00x. It is the one
  benchmark bound by RAM data access rather than instruction fetch, so it
  gets no wait-state benefit and simply pays the clock drop.
- Practical consequence: a DM42 on battery is a good deal less bad than the
  3.33x clock drop suggests, unless the work is a variable scan.
